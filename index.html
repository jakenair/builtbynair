Approach

Why a small studio that ships its own products is a different proposition from an agency.

You talk to the person writing the code

Carter takes the first call; Jake, who writes the code, is in the room for the brief. No account manager relaying messages, no requirements lost in a handoff, no junior developer quietly assigned after the pitch. The person who builds it is the person who answers when something breaks.

AI-assisted development, which changes the timeline

Modern AI tooling is a core part of how the work gets done, not an experiment running beside it. Scope a traditional shop quotes in quarters tends to land in weeks — and the pricing reflects that rather than quietly pocketing the difference.

Shipped, not delivered

A repository handed over at the end of a contract is not a product. We put the thing in front of real users, watch what breaks, and fix it — because we run our own platform on exactly the same terms.

We read the economics before writing the code

The background here is accounting, not just engineering. That means an honest answer about what a feature costs to run, and a willingness to say when the expensive version isn't worth building.

About

Jake Nair — Founder
Carter Nair — Founder

Built by Nair is Jake and Carter Nair, brothers based in Chicago. Two people: one who builds the software, one who works out what it needs to do.

Jake writes the code. He holds a bachelor's and a master's degree in accounting from the University of Missouri — an unusual route into writing production code, and one that turns out to be an advantage. He built TeeBox, a golf equipment marketplace, from nothing to a live platform with payment processing and an iOS app, single-handedly. Doing that alone meant learning the parts of shipping software most developers get to hand to someone else: payment compliance and identity verification, App Store review, email deliverability, and incident response at two in the morning when money is moving between strangers.

Most software gets built by people who have never had to run the thing afterwards. That changes what you build.

Carter handles business development and client relationships, and is usually the first person you speak to. He is a senior at North Central College in Naperville finishing a degree in computer science, with experience in cybersecurity and technology consulting. That matters more than it sounds: the person scoping your project can read the architecture, so nothing is promised in a first call that has to be walked back later. His job is working out what a project actually needs — and what it is worth — before a line of it gets quoted.

The accounting training shows up in the work. Before a feature gets built, the first question is what it costs to run and what it earns — which is why clients here tend to get talked out of at least one expensive idea per project.

We take on a small number of client builds at a time. What's for sale is attention, not a team's spare capacity.
